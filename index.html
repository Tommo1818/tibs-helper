Week 1 & 2 • Theme 0 & Theme 1

Epistemic Vigilance (Theme 0), Macro Paradigms (Perez) & Firm-Level Disruption (Christensen)

Theme 0 Preamble: "Don't Believe The Hype!" (Epistemic Vigilance)

Vendor-driven narratives frame every technological shift (AI, blockchain, quantum) as inevitable and unprecedented. Level 7 analysis requires a mental toolbox of competing analytical lenses: always ask who benefits from the hype, inspect historical precedents, and separate technical feasibility from commercial and institutional reality.

Core Theme 1 Contrast (Mandatory for 10/10 in Part B)

Carlota Perez operates at the macroeconomic / historical scale (50–60 year techno-economic surges driven by financial speculation bubbles and socio-institutional resets). Clayton Christensen operates at the microeconomic / firm-level scale (how rational incumbents overshoot customer demand and lose to low-end or new-market entrants).

2. Clayton Christensen (1997): The Innovator's Dilemma & Empirical Cases

Filter the performance trajectories below and review Christensen's empirical evidence (Disk Drives, Minimill Steel) and autonomous spin-off prescription.

Christensen's Core Mechanisms & Canonical Examples
  • Sustaining vs. Disruptive (3m): Sustaining innovation improves performance along metrics valued by mainstream customers. Disruptive innovation starts with inferior traditional performance, lower gross margins, and simpler/smaller form factors, establishing either a low-end foothold (overserved price-sensitive buyers) or a new-market foothold (turning non-consumers into consumers).
  • Performance Oversupply & Rational Failure (2m): Technological supply progresses faster than customer absorption capacity. Well-managed incumbents fail because they listen to their best, highest-margin customers and rational resource-allocation processes starve low-margin disruptive products.
  • Canonical Case 1 — Hard Disk Drives: 14-inch drives (mainframes) → 8-inch (minicomputers) → 5.25-inch (desktop PCs) → 3.5-inch (laptops). Each generation was initially dismissed by incumbent engineers for having lower capacity, before improving rapidly to capture the tier above.
  • Canonical Case 2 — Minimill Steel (Nucor vs. US Steel): Minimills entered via cheap, low-margin concrete reinforcing bar (rebar). Integrated steelmakers gladly ceded rebar to focus on high-margin automotive sheet steel — until minimills improved metallurgy and moved upmarket into structural beams and sheet steel.
  • Christensen's Prescription: An incumbent can rarely commercialise a disruptive technology inside its core unit; it must spin off an autonomous organisation whose cost structure is tailored to earn profits at low margins and small market sizes.

3. Level 7 Academic Critiques & Julian Birkinshaw's Strategic Response Matrix

Julian Birkinshaw — The 3 Myths of Disruption & 2×2 Matrix

Birkinshaw refutes three pervasive Silicon Valley myths: Myth 1 ("Every sector is under threat"): The Global 500 turnover rate has remained stable at ~10–12 companies/year since the 1970s; disruption clusters in TMT & retail while asset-heavy/regulated sectors (pharma, aerospace, banking) have deep moats. Myth 2 ("Disruption happens quickly"): Real transitions (EVs, cloud, ecommerce) take 20–30 years. Myth 3 ("Incumbents cannot adapt"): Incumbents deploy a 2×2 Offence/Defence Matrix:

PostureExisting MarketNew / Adjacent Market
Defensive Retrench: Focus on high-margin loyal segments where the firm holds an unassailable moat. Move Away: Exit commoditised low-margin segments and reallocate capital to new industries.
Offensive Double Down: Aggressively upgrade core technology and customer experience to neutralise challengers. Fight Back (Co-opt / Spin-In): Acquire the challenger, partner, or launch a competing disruptive business model.
Lepore, Shaughnessy, Weeks, Henderson & Clark, and Edgerton
  • Jill Lepore (2014, The New Yorker): Critiques disruption as a quasi-religious ideology replacing "progress". Exposes hand-picked survivorship bias (e.g., Seagate survived and dominated disk drives; Bucyrus Erie survived in excavators) and warns against panic-driven "auto-disruption" (destroying viable institutions out of fear).
  • Haydn Shaughnessy & Michael Weeks (2015): Expose the falsifiability / tautology trap (disruption is defined post-hoc by whether the incumbent lost) and the Apple iPhone anomaly — Christensen predicted in 2007 the iPhone would fail as a high-end sustaining device against Nokia, missing how platform ecosystems (iOS App Store) disrupt from the top down.
  • Henderson & Clark (1990): Distinguish modular innovation (changing components) from architectural innovation (reconfiguring how existing components link together, which breaks an incumbent's internal communication channels).
  • David Edgerton (2006, The Shock of the Old): Critiques innovation-centric bias — global welfare depends far more on humble technologies-in-use and maintenance (corrugated iron, shipping containers, bicycles) than hyped novelties. Since most R&D ideas fail, good R&D governance requires terminating unviable projects early.

Rubric Breakdown (10m Theory + 10m Bank Application)
  • Why Conflict is Inevitable (2m): The Performance Engine is engineered for repeatable efficiency, predictable cash flows, and strict compliance KPIs; innovation is non-routine and uncertain.
  • Why Lone Rebels Fail (2m): The Performance Engine can only run incremental improvements on its own; lone "rebel" intrapreneurs fighting entrenched organisational processes get crushed.
  • Organising Model (3m): Project Team = Dedicated Team + Shared Staff. Shared Staff handle tasks that fit existing routines; the Dedicated Team is built from scratch (outside hires, new roles, distinct metrics) and protected by a senior supervising executive.

Week 4 • Theme 3

Organising R&D (PARC vs. Open Innovation) & Financing (Mazzucato vs. Westlake)

Two Core Enquiries: (1) How should firms structure R&D? (Centralised corporate labs vs. Chesbrough's Open Innovation vs. O'Reilly & Tushman's Structural Ambidexterity). (2) Who takes the real risk in financing breakthrough technologies? (Mariana Mazzucato's Entrepreneurial State vs. Stian Westlake's critique, and Eric Ries's Lean Startup vs. Reid Hoffman's Blitzscaling).

1. Organising Innovation: Centralised Corporate Labs vs. Open Innovation vs. Ambidexterity

Centralised R&D Labs
Bell Labs, Xerox PARC & The HP Way

Strengths: Long-term patient capital insulated from quarterly profit pressures enabled fundamental breakthroughs (Bell Labs: transistor, laser, UNIX, information theory; Xerox PARC: GUI, bitmapped display, Ethernet, laser printing).

Why Xerox Failed to Capture Value from the GUI:
1. Core Rigidity: Xerox executives viewed the firm strictly as a paper copier business.
2. Architectural Filtering (Henderson & Clark, 1990): Communication channels filtered out non-copier architectures.
3. Complementary Assets & Appropriability (Teece, 1986): Xerox lacked consumer software ecosystems and allowed Apple/Microsoft to appropriate the paradigm.

Open Innovation
Henry Chesbrough (2003) & Enkel et al. (2020)

Core Premise: "Not all the smart people work for us." Replaces the closed internal R&D funnel with permeable firm boundaries, shifting from R&D to Acquisition-and-Development (A&D).

Inbound Open Innovation: Licensing external university/startup IP, corporate venture capital (CVC), crowdsourcing.
Outbound Open Innovation: Spinning out non-core technologies so they do not sit on the shelf like PARC's GUI, or open-sourcing frameworks (Google open-sourcing TensorFlow & Kubernetes) to commoditise complements and set industry standards.

Structural Ambidexterity
Defining the Hybrid Boundary (Slide 6 Rule)

Seminar Warning: Never just write "use a hybrid model" — you must explicitly specify what stays internal vs. what goes external.

O'Reilly & Tushman Framework: Maintain structural ambidexterity by separating exploitative units (incremental efficiency) from exploratory units (radical R&D), linked at the senior executive level. Keep proprietary model weights, safety-critical architectures, and high-appropriability IP internal; use external open innovation for application ecosystems, peripheral tooling, and market speed.

2. Financing Innovation: The Valley of Death & Mazzucato vs. Westlake

The Core Policy Debate: Socialising Risks vs. Privatising Rewards

Mariana Mazzucato (2013, The Entrepreneurial State) & Rutger Bregman (2017): Debunks the myth that the state is merely a bureaucratic fixer of market failures. Demonstrates that every foundational technology inside the Apple iPhone — the Internet (DARPA), GPS (US DoD), multi-touch screens (NSF/CIA-funded university research), and Siri (DARPA) — was funded at high-risk TRL 1–4 by mission-oriented public agencies. Critiques private VC for entering only after the state has absorbed the fundamental uncertainty, thereby "socialising risks while privatising rewards" (proposing public equity stakes or income-contingent royalties).

Stian Westlake (2014, Nesta Critique):
1. Narrow Science/Hardware Bias: Mazzucato equates innovation with laboratory R&D, ignoring massive private investment in intangible assets (UX design, supply-chain orchestration, branding, business models).
2. State Survivorship Bias: Highlights successful public missions while ignoring expensive government commercial failures (Concorde supersonic jet, Minitel, Anglo-French breeder reactors).
3. Downstream Commercial Risk: Having raw components (GPS, Internet) does not make commercialising a global smartphone ecosystem risk-free.

3. Startup Execution & Scaling: Kawasaki, Ries (Lean Startup) & Hoffman (Blitzscaling)

Guy Kawasaki — The Art of the Start 2.0

Make Meaning, Not Just Money: Replace hollow corporate mission statements with a 3-word mantra (e.g., "Empowering athletic performance"). Jump to the Next Curve: True innovation jumps from ice-harvesting (1.0) → ice factory (2.0) → personal refrigerator (3.0). Build products that are DICEE (Deep, Intelligent, Complete, Empowering, Elegant).

Eric Ries — The Lean Startup (2011)

Validated Learning under Uncertainty: Treat every startup strategy as a falsifiable hypothesis. Deploy a Minimum Viable Product (MVP) through rapid Build–Measure–Learn feedback loops to decide whether to Pivot or Persevere. Critique: Over-reliance on customer feedback can trap firms in incremental local maxima rather than radical architectural leaps.

Reid Hoffman — Blitzscaling (2016) vs. Kuratko et al. (2020)

Speed Over Efficiency: In winner-take-all markets with strong two-sided network effects, burn capital aggressively to secure first-scaler monopoly. Kuratko, Holt & Neubert (2020) (The Good, The Bad, and The Ugly): Contrast valid blitzscaling (Flexport) with catastrophic unit-economics and governance/ethical collapse when applied without true network moats (WeWork, Uber, Zenefits, Theranos).

IP Regimes, Economic Theory & Required Talis Case Law
IP RightScope & DurationLandmark Case & Legal Doctrine
Patents Functional invention (novel, inventive step, industrial application). Territorial, ~20 yrs in exchange for public disclosure (Utilitarian Bargain). Brin & Page (1998) Paper vs. US6285999B1: Publishing an academic paper does NOT place the method in the public domain if patented! In UK/EU, "software as such" is excluded unless it produces a further technical effect. Beware Heller & Eisenberg's Tragedy of the Anticommons (patent thickets blocking downstream innovation).
Copyright Original expression of an idea (source code as literary work, images, prose). Automatic, Life + 70 yrs. Temple Island Collections v New English Teas [2012] ("Red Bus Case"): Copyright never protects an idea (a red bus on Westminster Bridge), but copying the specific visual composition and monochrome background contrast constitutes unlawful substantial reproduction. Also note Google v. Oracle (fair use of declaring API code).
Trade Secrets Confidential commercial info (proprietary datasets, model weights, formulas). Indefinite while secret; does not block independent reverse engineering. Faccenda Chicken v Fowler [1986] & Vestergaard v Bestnet [2013]: Distinguishes an employee's general skill and knowledge (portable to a new job) from high-grade confidential trade secrets and employer-owned source code (which cannot be taken to a startup).

2. Open Source Software: Raymond's Bazaar (1999) vs. Eghbal's Infrastructure Fragility (2020)

Optimistic Thesis
Eric Raymond — The Cathedral and the Bazaar

Contrasts hierarchical, closed-team Cathedral releases with decentralised, peer-reviewed Bazaar collaboration (Linux kernel). Formulates Linus's Law: "Given enough eyeballs, all bugs are shallow." Assumes user growth naturally brings proportional developer contributions.

Empirical Reality Check
Nadia Eghbal — Working in Public (2020)

Classifies open-source projects by contributor vs. user growth: while Linux is a rare Federation (high contributors, high users), most critical packages are Stadiums (1–2 solo unpaid maintainers supporting millions of passive consumers). Exposes severe security fragility: OpenSSL Heartbleed (2014), Apache Log4j / Log4Shell (2021), and the XZ Utils social-engineering backdoor (2024).

Licensing Architecture
Permissive vs. Copyleft & Creative Commons

Permissive (MIT, BSD, Apache 2.0): Allows modification and bundling inside proprietary closed-source products (Apache 2.0 adds an explicit patent grant).
Copyleft / Viral (GPLv2, GPLv3, AGPL): Requires any distributed derivative work (or network service under AGPL) to release its full source code under the same GPL licence.
Creative Commons: CC BY (attribution) vs. CC BY-NC (strictly forbids commercial use).

3. EU AI Act (Regulation 2024/1689), UK GDPR / DUAA 2025 & Seminar Case Studies (Slides 9, 10 & 13)

Interactive EU AI Act Risk Pyramid & Regulatory Pillars
Applied Seminar Scenarios (Slides 9, 10 & 13)
  • UK GDPR Art. 5 Seven Principles: (1) Lawfulness, fairness & transparency; (2) Purpose limitation; (3) Data minimisation; (4) Accuracy; (5) Storage limitation; (6) Integrity & confidentiality; (7) Accountability.
  • Slide 9 Case (UK Online Retailer Fraud Model): Retailer repurposes shopping history to automatically close customer accounts for suspected refund abuse, with a staff member briefly clicking "approve".
    Legal Diagnosis: Repurposing data triggers purpose limitation & transparency checks. A staff member rubber-stamping outputs without investigating evidence does not make the decision human — it remains a solely automated decision with significant effect under UK DUAA 2025, requiring proactive notice, customer representations, and genuine human intervention (tested via override rates, review duration logs, and blind seeded test cases).
  • Slide 10 Case (EU Road Authority AI Traffic-Signal Controller): An EU road authority deploys an AI system controlling traffic signals on a busy urban corridor.
    Legal Diagnosis: Classified as High-Risk AI under Annex III Point 2 (Critical Infrastructure: road traffic management safety components). Under Teichmann, severe physical residual harm to pedestrians demands strict ex-ante fault-tree testing and manual fail-safe overrides. Under Ebers (2024), regulators must also weigh congestion/emission reductions and allow phased shadow-mode trials rather than blocking deployment indefinitely.
